Why the boxes split

You do not build “the agent” — you build three writers and a checker, each its own node. You do not build “SharePoint” — you build three lists that exist and three more places the new flow needs: a prompt library, Contoso's deep dive sources, and a library for the finished issue.

The eight stories are rows in one list, not more columns. That is why there is one Stories box and not eight.

The checker sits in the middle

Trace headline from the announcement writer and it forks: straight into Stories, and into the fact-check, whose verdict lands on the same row. Check first, then write — no row ever exists unchecked.

Prompts and Contoso's own deep dive material never reach the checker. There is nothing to verify.

SharePoint · what to build, in order

Every list and library

Seven places on the Contoso Ai Monthly Newsletter site. Three exist, four are new. Work through the pills left to right. Each shows exactly what to add or change, with a Copy button on every name and value.

One rule for every new column: type its name exactly as shown the first time you create it. SharePoint freezes the first name as the hidden name forever, and the flows and the app use the hidden name. Rename it later and they break.

What happens when you press Run.
  1. The app creates the month in the Issues list, and eight rows in Stories — slot, position and seed.
  2. The flow works through the rows. A library prompt is copied in as it is. Agent writes one goes to the prompt writer. An Contoso file deep dive is written from that file. A research deep dive and every announcement are found on the web and fact-checked before they are written.
  3. Each story is written into its row, with its verdict. Four spare announcements are added.
  4. Screen 2 opens: your edits on the left, the finished email on the right.

Does everything tie together?

Three checks, run against the trace, the map and the schedule as they stand. If any of them is not empty, a piece is missing from a tab, a field has no writer or no reader, or one piece goes by two names.

1 · Every piece, on every tab

2 · Every field has a writer and a reader

A SharePoint column needs something that writes it and something that reads it, unless it is marked you — edited by you directly in SharePoint — or kept for the record, which needs a writer but no reader. An agent's input needs something that feeds it. Fields marked as going nowhere on purpose are not counted.

3 · One name per piece

4 · Your flows

Read from the Flows tab as you edit it. These are notes on your workbench, not failures of the build, so they do not change the line at the top.
